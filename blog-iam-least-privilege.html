Getting to least-privilege IAM without breaking everything

2026-02-10 · 6 min read

Over-permissioned IAM roles are one of the most common findings in any AWS Security Hub or Prisma Cloud scan, and one of the scariest to fix. The risk isn't writing a tighter policy — it's writing one that's too tight and quietly breaking a production workflow at 2 AM. Here's the approach that let me tighten dozens of roles in a live, regulated account without a single incident.

Start with access patterns, not assumptions

Before touching a single policy, I pulled actual usage data from IAM Access Analyzer and CloudTrail. This tells you what a role actually does, as opposed to what its policy allows it to do — and the gap between those two is usually enormous. Most roles I reviewed used under 15% of their granted permissions.

Tackle one role at a time, in non-prod first

It's tempting to batch this work, but a single overly broad change can ripple across every service that assumes a shared role. I worked role-by-role, validating each tightened policy in staging before promoting it. Where possible, I split shared roles into per-service roles — more roles to manage, but each one is independently auditable and blast-radius is contained.

Use SCPs and permission boundaries as guardrails

Rather than relying purely on individual role policies, I layered in Service Control Policies at the organization level and permission boundaries at the role level. This means even if a future policy edit is too permissive, the boundary caps what it can actually do — a safety net that catches human error before it becomes an incident. 

{
  "Effect": "Deny",
  "Action": [
    "iam:CreateUser",
    "iam:CreateAccessKey"
  ],
  "Resource": "*",
  "Condition": {
    "StringNotEquals": {
      "aws:RequestedRegion": "ap-south-1"
    }
  }
}

Rotate, don't just restrict

Tightening permissions is half the story — the other half is making sure long-lived credentials don't exist to be over-permissioned in the first place. Migrating static keys into AWS Secrets Manager with automatic rotation closed off an entire class of findings, since there was simply nothing left to leak.

The result

Across this effort, we cleared 70+ Well-Architected Review high-risk issues and over 600 findings from Security Hub and Prisma Cloud — all without a production incident. The playbooks from this work were adopted by three other teams, cutting their average remediation turnaround in half.
